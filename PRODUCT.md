# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two distinct audiences on two surfaces that deliberately feel different:

**Walk-in customers** use the public sign-up / order flow — often on their own phone, standing at the counter, with seconds of patience. They enter a few details, optionally opt into WhatsApp, and may order items. This surface is warm, fast, and operational: it has to survive a real walk-in and reward them immediately (a WhatsApp welcome, a placed order).

**Business owners and staff** live in the dashboard. They see who signed up, what sold, who needs re-engaging, and act on it — logging sales, running campaigns, working segments and journeys, reading AI-surfaced findings. This surface should be pleasant to sit in for a shift: comfortable, legible, advisory rather than alarming.

The two surfaces share data but not tone; keep the customer flow's speed-and-trust needs and the dashboard's comfort-and-insight needs from bleeding into each other.

## Product Purpose

rice-mice is a lean CRM + POS for small food businesses. It captures new-customer sign-ups at the point of sale — where that data otherwise sits in a paper notebook or nowhere — stores contacts and transactions in one place, and gives owners a live dashboard plus AI assistance to turn first-time buyers into repeat customers over WhatsApp, the channel these businesses actually use. Success is a customer who signs up in seconds and shows up in the dashboard immediately, and an owner who can see who to bring back and act on it without leaving the app.

## Brand Personality

The warmth of the food business itself, expressed two ways. The customer sign-up/order flow is warm, quick, and confidence-building — it feels like being greeted, not processed. The owner dashboard is calm and advisory: AI recommends, advises, and raises notable findings, but the room stays comfortable and easy on the eyes, a place an owner is happy to spend a shift. Intelligence that reassures rather than nags; a warm-orange palette that reads as hospitality, not software.

## Anti-references

Not a cold POS terminal — no sterile grey checkout-machine feel on the sign-up/order flow; the business's warmth has to come through. Not a generic AI SaaS template — no cream background, purple gradients, or tracked-uppercase eyebrows, especially around the AI elements.

*Amended Sprint 56 after an audit raised both against the shipped code.* **Violet is kept for the AI layer** — the analyst rail, agentic proposals, "Ask why", canvas message nodes — as a deliberate owner decision. The intent behind the anti-reference is "don't look like a template"; a single flat accent that marks *which parts of the screen are the assistant* earns its place, and the ban was meant for purple **gradients** and decorative chrome, not a functional accent. Solid violet stays; violet gradients remain out. See the matching narrowing of DESIGN.md's No-Eyebrow Rule. Not an anxious, alarm-heavy dashboard — alerts and findings must never make the owner feel nagged or stressed; comfort beats a wall of red badges.

## Design Principles

Two audiences, two tempos. The customer flow optimizes for seconds and trust; the dashboard optimizes for comfort and insight. Design each for its own user; don't average them into one look.

Capture at the counter. The sign-up and order flow must hold up for a real walk-in on a phone — fast, warm, forgiving of mistakes, rewarding on submit.

Calm intelligence. AI advises and surfaces findings without alarming. Notable events are offered, not shouted; the default emotional register is reassurance.

Warmth of the shop, not the vendor. It should feel like the food business it serves — hospitable and human — never like enterprise software dropped on top of it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA: ≥4.5:1 body-text contrast, keyboard navigation, always-visible focus, and reduced-motion alternatives. This matters doubly on the public sign-up/order form, which must work on any customer's phone, in any lighting, first try.
